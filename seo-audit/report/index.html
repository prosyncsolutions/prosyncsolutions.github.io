The short version

The site has good bones: clear service pages, a sensible menu, and every page loads. But a few configuration mistakes are hiding some of its most valuable pages from Google, and that matters far more than any amount of new content.

3 · Detailed findings

What's wrong, why it matters, and the fix

1. The blog is blocked from search engines

Critical
Evidence
robots.txt contains Disallow: /blog/. Affected: /blog/ and /blog/desk-stretches.html. The article is also missing from sitemap.xml.
Why it matters
Blog posts like “5 desk stretches for office workers” are how a local clinic gets found by people who aren't searching for a physio yet. Right now Google is told not to read any of them.
Fix
Delete the Disallow: /blog/ line, add every blog post to the sitemap, then request indexing in Google Search Console.

2. Services page canonical points to the home page

Critical
Evidence
/services.html has <link rel="canonical" href="/">.
Why it matters
A canonical tag says “this page is a copy; rank the other one instead.” Google treats it as a strong hint, so the Services page, one of the site's main money pages, is likely to be left out of results.
Fix
Change it to a self-referencing canonical: <link rel="canonical" href="https://www.brightwaterphysio.example/services.html">. Usually caused by a theme or plugin template copying the home page's settings, so check other templates too.

3. Prices page is hidden from Google and from visitors

High
Evidence
/pricing.html has <meta name="robots" content="noindex">, is listed in the sitemap, and has 0 internal links pointing to it.
Why it matters
Searches like “physio prices Bristol” come from people close to booking. The sitemap asks Google to index the page while the page itself says don't, which sends mixed signals. Visitors can't find it either, because nothing on the site links to it.
Fix
Remove the noindex tag (likely left over from before launch), and link to Prices from the main menu and from each service page.

4. Contact page isn't set up for mobile

High
Evidence
/contact.html has no <meta name="viewport"> tag.
Why it matters
Phones render the page at desktop width and shrink it, so text is tiny and the phone number is hard to tap. Google ranks based on the mobile version of pages, and this is the page where bookings happen.
Fix
Add <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"> to the page template.

5. Services and About share the same title and description

High
Evidence
Both pages are titled Brightwater Physio with an identical meta description.
Why it matters
The title is the blue link people click in search results and one of the strongest clues to what a page is about. A bare business name wastes that on both pages and gives searchers no reason to click.
Fix
Services: Physio & Sports Massage Services in Bristol | Brightwater (57 chars). About: About Our Chartered Physiotherapists | Brightwater Physio (57 chars). Write a unique description for each.

6. Home page title too long, no description

High
Evidence
Title is 95 characters: Brightwater Physio | Physiotherapy, Sports Massage and Rehab Clinic in Bristol, UK - Book Today. No meta description.
Why it matters
Google cuts titles off at roughly 580 pixels (about 55–60 characters), so the end gets lost. Without a description, Google builds the snippet from page text it judges relevant. It often rewrites descriptions anyway, but a good one gives you a say in the first message searchers read.
Fix
Title: Physiotherapy Clinic in Bristol | Brightwater Physio (52 chars). Description (about 150 chars) covering what you treat, where, and how to book.

7. Broken links

Medium
Evidence
/about.html links to /team.html, which returns 404. The sitemap lists /old-offers.html, which returns 404.
Why it matters
The broken Team link is a dead end on a page people visit to decide whether to trust you. The sitemap error won't hurt rankings (Google just drops the dead URL), but it clutters Search Console's reports so real problems are harder to spot.
Fix
Either build the Team page (good for trust) or remove the link. Remove /old-offers.html from the sitemap, or redirect it (301) to Prices if it ever had backlinks.

8. 1.8 MB hero image

High
Evidence
/img/clinic-hero.png is 1,820 KB, saved as PNG at 1800×900, with no alt text.
Why it matters
It's the first thing that loads. On a mobile connection it can add seconds before the page looks ready, which hurts Core Web Vitals (LCP) and bookings.
Fix
Export as AVIF or WebP at about 1600px wide (target: under 200 KB), add srcset for phones, and add descriptive alt text.

9–14. Content and markup clean-up

Medium / Low
Thin pages
/sports-injury.html has 67 words, no description and a broken image (/img/knee-taping.jpg, 404). /back-pain.html (266) and /services.html (209) cover the basics but don't answer common questions about cost, number of sessions or insurance.
Headings
Contact has no H1; change “Get in touch” to an H1 so the page has a clear main heading for visitors and screen readers.
Accessibility
Images missing alt text on / and /about.html; /contact.html missing lang="en-GB".
Fix
Grow each service page to answer the 5–6 questions patients actually ask (taken from Search Console queries and front-desk calls), with an FAQ section.
4 · Judgment calls

Things the tools flagged that I'm not prioritising

Crawlers flag anything outside a rule of thumb. Part of an audit is saying which warnings don't matter, so your hours go where they count.

  • Two H1 headings on the home page. Google has said for years that multiple H1s are fine. I'd tidy it during the redesign of that section, but it won't change rankings.
  • Back pain and Contact titles at 61 characters. Google truncates by pixel width, not character count. These are borderline, not broken. Low priority.
  • Word count on Contact (84), Blog index (39) and Prices (120). These pages aren't meant to be long. Word count isn't a ranking factor by itself; what matters is whether the page answers the searcher. Only the service pages are flagged as thin.
  • Server response time. The crawl ran against a local test server, so speed numbers from this crawl don't reflect real hosting. In a live engagement, speed is measured with PageSpeed Insights and Search Console's Core Web Vitals report, which use real-visitor data.
5 · Plan

30 / 60 / 90 days

Days 1–30: Fix & measure

  • Items 1–8 from the priority list
  • Verify Google Search Console & GA4, and submit a clean sitemap
  • Record a baseline: clicks, impressions, indexed pages, Core Web Vitals
  • Google Business Profile check: categories, hours, photos, phone matching the site

Days 31–60: Content

  • Keyword research from Search Console queries and local competitors
  • Rebuild Sports injury, Back pain and Services pages around real patient questions
  • Add FAQs, internal links to Prices and Contact
  • Two blog posts targeting questions people already search for

Days 61–90: Authority

  • Local citations: NHS-adjacent and health directories, Bristol business listings
  • Outreach to local running clubs, gyms and employers (sponsorships, guest advice pieces)
  • Review request process after discharge
  • Re-crawl and compare against the baseline

No promise of a specific traffic number: nobody can honestly guarantee rankings. Every hour is tied to a specific task you'll see in the monthly report.

7 · Method & limits

How this audit was produced

The site was crawled with a custom Python crawler (seo_crawler.py, standard library only) that follows every internal link from the home page, reads robots.txt and sitemap.xml, then runs these checks:

Status codes · broken internal links & images · robots.txt blocking · sitemap errors & gaps · noindex conflicts · orphan pages · canonical tags · titles (missing, long, duplicate) · meta descriptions (missing, length, duplicate) · H1 structure · word count · image alt text & weight · viewport · language attribute

Raw output: pages.csv (one row per URL) and findings.json. The crawler was tested against a list of 19 deliberately planted problems and detected all 19.

Not covered by a crawl

  • Real-visitor speed (Core Web Vitals field data): from PageSpeed Insights / Search Console
  • Backlinks and competitor gaps: from Search Console links report and a backlink tool
  • Actual search queries and rankings: from Search Console once access is granted
  • JavaScript-rendered content: this crawler reads the HTML as served
